If AI is the engine, data is the fuel

Every AI tool in this course was built the same way underneath: by learning patterns from data. Not from a textbook, and not from years of clinical training — from thousands, sometimes millions, of examples fed into it during development.

💡 The core idea: an AI model doesn't know anything you haven't shown it. If the data it learned from was incomplete, wrong, or unrepresentative, the model inherits those gaps — and it won't know what it doesn't know.
What this step covers

Three things you'll build a working understanding of, each with its own hands-on activity:

1. The types of health data — structured, unstructured and semi-structured — and why most AI tools are trained overwhelmingly on the easiest kind.

2. When data goes wrong — missing, inaccurate and inappropriate data, and how each one quietly misleads a model trained on it.

3. How the UK governs health data — UK GDPR, the Caldicott Principles and the Goldacre Review — and why that governance exists.

Three things to keep in mind
🚫 "Garbage in, garbage out" is not a throwaway line. It's one of the biggest reasons AI tools that look impressive in a lab underperform, or actively mislead, once they meet real patients.
🚫 Most AI tools train on structured data. Free text carries huge clinical nuance, but converting it to something a model can use always loses detail.
🚫 Governance isn't bureaucracy for its own sake. Data this sensitive, powering tools this consequential, has to be handled properly — that's what UK GDPR, Caldicott and the Goldacre Review are for.

Key takeaways

🗂️
Data comes in different forms. Structured, unstructured and semi-structured data all need different handling — and most AI tools lean heavily on the structured kind.
⚠️
Flawed data produces flawed AI. Missing, inaccurate and inappropriate data don't just create noise — they become the bias and blind spots covered in Week 2.
⚖️
Governance protects patients and enables trust. UK GDPR, the Caldicott Principles and the Goldacre Review exist because health data is uniquely sensitive and uniquely powerful.
